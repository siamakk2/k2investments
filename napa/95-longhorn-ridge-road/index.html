Ninety-five Longhorn Ridge sits atop the mountain landscape of the same gated equestrian community — three parcels totalling just over 78 acres, wrapped in oak and open country, with a porch that looks straight at Mt. Tamalpais.

Inside, high ceilings and deep natural light do most of the work. Stone tile flooring and a wood-burning fireplace anchor the living room. The kitchen has a butcher-block centre island against dark stone counters, a pot filler over the gas range and integrated sound. Upstairs: the primary suite, a loft overlooking the living room, and a bonus room.

The land is the headline. Seasonal pond, barn, horse stalls, multiple pastures, walking trails and vineyard and livestock potential across three parcels. In a county where hillside and agricultural protections sharply limit what can be built and subdivided, usable acreage of this size is a shrinking subset of an already thin market.

Questions

What buyers ask.

How many parcels make up the property?

Three legal parcels totalling approximately 78 acres, held together as one offering.

What are the views?

Mt. Tamalpais and Mt. George on the skyline, with canyons, forests, pastures and valleys in between.

What equestrian facilities are there?

A barn, horse stalls, multiple fenced pastures, and walking and riding trails, plus a seasonal pond.

Is there vineyard potential?

The land carries vineyard and livestock potential across its three parcels, subject to Napa County requirements. Any planting should be confirmed with the county during due diligence.
