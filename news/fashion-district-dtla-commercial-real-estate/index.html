A district built on immigrant entrepreneurship

The Fashion District’s modern identity took shape in the late 1970s, when a handful of jobbers began working the blocks around Santee Alley — buying leftover textiles and remnant fabric, turning them into simple garments, and selling to swap marts and small retailers. Through the 1980s and 1990s that jobber model grew alongside a wave of Korean-American entrepreneurship, as garment-business owners built a tightly networked ecosystem of importers, sewing contractors, manufacturers and wholesalers — many with direct supply lines to South Korea’s textile industry.

That history is still visible today. The LA Fashion District employs an estimated 25,000 Korean and Korean-American workers, roughly 13% of California’s entire fashion-related workforce.

Sources: Metropolitics, on ethnic solidarity in the Los Angeles Korean garment district; The Korea Times, on Korean employment in the LA Fashion District.

Why investors and owner-users choose this district

  • Central location, walkable density. The Fashion District sits inside greater Downtown LA, minutes from the Financial District, the Arts District and the 10/110/101 interchange.
  • A working, revenue-generating district. Unlike purely speculative submarkets, the Fashion District has an active daytime economy — wholesale, garment manufacturing, creative and production tenants — that supports real lease demand, not just a bet on land appreciation.
  • Adaptive reuse inventory. Much of the building stock, including our own 530 E 8th Street listings, is early-twentieth-century industrial and loft space well suited to creative office, showroom and light-industrial conversion.
