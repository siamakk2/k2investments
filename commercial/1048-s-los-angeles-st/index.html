Ideal for 1031 exchange buyers, owner-users, international investors and private capital.

Building history

A century of history, rebuilt for the next owner.

Most Downtown buildings of this vintage carry a hundred years of deferred maintenance. This one does not. The 2009 rebuild replaced every major system under permit, and the record below is documented with LADBS permit numbers rather than described in adjectives.

1922

Original construction

Two-storey brick commercial building, 60 × 150 ft, permitted for manufacturing and retail use under Permit 1922LA16063. The historic brick shell has been preserved through every subsequent rebuild.

1984

Seismic safety retrofit

Earthquake anchor-bolt work carried out as a Division 88, Alteration II Class II partial-compliance retrofit, consistent with Downtown Los Angeles building standards of the era.

2008

Fire damage and stabilisation

The structure sustained extensive fire damage (CSR 196669, June 2008). The building was braced and secured, and methane hazard mitigation was approved with a sub-slab vent system installed to LADBS requirements.

2009

Full permitted rebuild — all systems replaced

A comprehensive rebuild covering elevator rails, lobby, electrical rooms, masonry infill and all deferred work. New electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roof, structure, bathrooms and elevator were installed, fully permitted through LADBS under Permit 09016-10000-07001. This is the work that resets the building’s effective age.

2019

Electrical upgrade

Express electrical permit 19041-90000-48920 — dedicated outlet installed by licensed contractor Brennan Electric under Naninam LLC ownership.

2026

Listed for sale

Exclusively listed at $4,950,000 by Bobak Kalhor, K2 Investment, Inc., DRE #01198998. Listing period 15 July 2026 to 15 January 2027.

Questions

What buyers ask.

What is the cap rate and NOI?

A 5.08% cap rate on approximately $251,516 of 2025 net operating income, at the $4,950,000 asking price — $325.04 per square foot across 15,229 square feet.

Where is the upside?

The building is fully leased, but rents have not been materially increased in about ten years. The spread between in-place and market rents in the Fashion District is the value-add thesis, and month-to-month tenancy means you do not have to wait out long leases to capture it.

What work has been done to the building?

Following a 2009 fire the property was comprehensively rebuilt — new electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roof, structure, bathrooms and elevator. Plans, permits and final inspections are available for review.

Does it qualify for a 1031 exchange?

Yes. It is stabilised, income-producing commercial property that qualifies as like-kind replacement property. K2 has extensive experience with the 45-day identification and 180-day closing timeline.

Can an owner-user occupy space?

Yes. Because tenancy is primarily month-to-month, an owner-user has unusual flexibility to take space over time while continuing to collect income on the balance of the building.
